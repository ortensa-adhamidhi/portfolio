LAB IQ — Multi-Branch Laboratory Analytics

A seven-dashboard Tableau reporting suite for a national laboratory network, built with role-based row-level security so doctors, branch managers, and executives each see exactly the data their role requires.

01 / The Challenge

Turnaround time was invisible above the branch level

LAB IQ operates a large laboratory network across 25+ branches nationwide, processing over 12 million tests a year across routine and urgent workflows. Each branch could see its own results, but nobody had a consolidated view of turnaround time (TAT) performance, sample rejection patterns, or workload distribution across the network.

On top of that, reporting access needed to respect a strict clinical hierarchy: doctors should only ever see their own patients' data, branch managers should see their own branch's operational metrics, and senior/overall managers need full network-wide visibility to compare branches and spot systemic bottlenecks — all from the same underlying model, without duplicating dashboards per role.

02 / Approach

A governed model with role-based access built in

I built a Tableau reporting suite of seven interconnected dashboards on top of the laboratory information system, modeled around the metrics that actually drive operational decisions: turnaround time against target, sample acceptance and rejection, approval automation rates, and workload distribution by branch, test group, and referring source.

Row-level security was implemented so a single published data source powers every role's view:

👨‍⚕️ Doctors

See only the tests and results tied to their own patients — no visibility into other providers' cases or branch-wide figures.

🏥 Branch Managers

See full operational detail for their own branch — TAT, rejection rates, workload — with no access to other branches' data.

📊 Overall Managers

See network-wide performance across all branches, with side-by-side comparison to identify outliers and systemic issues.

03 / What's In the Suite

Seven dashboards covering the full testing lifecycle

From sample intake through result approval, each stage of the lab workflow has a dedicated view:

  • Sample Acceptance Analysis — rejection rates by reason and referring source, tracked against a network-wide target
  • Workload Analysis — test volume by branch, test group, and referring institution, with hourly/daily load heatmaps
  • TAT Analysis & TAT Detail Analysis — turnaround time against routine and urgent targets, broken down by test, hour, and day of week
  • Duration Comparison — month-over-month TAT trends and delay-rate trends by test group
  • Laboratory Comparison — side-by-side branch benchmarking on TAT, volume, and target attainment
  • Approval Analysis — automated vs. manual approval rates, broken down by test group and urgency
04 / Result

Network-wide visibility, without breaking access boundaries

The network now runs on a single governed reporting layer instead of siloed, branch-level views — with routine TAT targets met 99.2% of the time and urgent TAT targets met 94.4% of the time, both now visible and comparable across every branch. Sample rejection sits at roughly 1.2% of submitted samples, broken down by reason and source so branches can act on the biggest drivers first.
